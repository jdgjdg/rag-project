## Cardinality of Relationships in ER Diagrams

Previously in the course, we discussed the concept of cardinality of relationships and how to notate it in ER diagrams. We have different kinds of cardinality: one-to-one, many-to-one, and many-to-many relationships.

The notation in ER diagrams that accompanies these cardinalities is added to the edges of the relationship.

*   **One-to-One:** Each one of these maps to one of those.
*   **Many-to-One:** Each one of these maps to one of those.
*   **One-to-Many:** Each one of these maps to many of those.

For example, a relationship where each one of these maps to one of those, and each one of these maps to many of those, would be represented accordingly.

Conventionally, different letters are used to denote the number of mappings in each direction (e.g., 'n' and 'm') to emphasize that these numbers do not have to be the same.

### Example with Cardinality Information

Let's examine a familiar example with added cardinality information:

*   **Product and Manufacturer:** Each product is made by one manufacturer, while a manufacturer makes many products (zero or more).
*   **Product and Buyer:** A product can be bought by many people, and each person can buy many products (many-to-many).
*   **Person and Manufacturer:** A person works at one manufacturer, but each manufacturer employs many people (many-to-one).

It is important to note that even if the letter 'n' is used multiple times, it does not represent a variable with the same value each time; it is simply a shorthand for "many." Other notations use the infinity symbol instead.

## Minimum Cardinality (Participation)

Along with maximum cardinality, there is also minimum cardinality, sometimes called participation. This provides information not covered by maximum cardinality.

Consider an example with only maximum cardinality: A product is made by one manufacturer. This allows for the possibility of products that are not yet made by any company. In this diagram, a product is made by at most one manufacturer, and a manufacturer makes at most an arbitrary number of products. It is permissible for a manufacturer to make no products.

If we want to specify that a product *must* be made by a company, we can indicate this in the diagram. A product is made by at least one manufacturer and at most by one manufacturer. Conversely, a manufacturer makes at least zero products and at most an arbitrary number of products.

A common shorthand is that "zero to n" is the same as "n." This shorthand will be frequently used in future diagrams.

## Considerations for Assigning Cardinalities

When creating an entity-relationship diagram and assigning cardinalities, it is crucial to remember that the real world is complex, and cardinalities are often higher than initially expected.

**Examples of Real-World Complexity:**

*   **Capitals:** A country typically has one capital. However, some countries have two (e.g., for executive and legislative branches), and during wartime, there can be competing capitals.
*   **Heads of State:** Each country usually has a single head of state. However, countries like Andorra have two heads of state. Historically, individuals like Simon Bolivar have served as president of multiple countries simultaneously.

These examples illustrate that relationships are not always neat and can frequently result in many-to-many relationships in diagrams.

## Allowed Cardinalities

A common question is whether cardinalities other than zero and one are allowed. The answer is no; they are not permitted in this notation.

We limit ourselves to cardinalities of zero and one for two primary reasons:

1.  **Semantics:** Constants other than zero and one rarely represent fundamental, unchangeable restrictions. For instance, the number of players on a sports team (e.g., Rugby with 15, 13, or 7 players per side) can vary and is not a fundamental, fixed restriction.
2.  **Pragmatics:** Implementing cardinalities of zero and one, as well as "many," is straightforward in SQL. However, implementing other specific cardinalities like seven or fifteen is not as easily supported.

When specific, non-standard cardinalities arise, there are a couple of ways to model them:

*   **Generalization:** A one-to-five relationship can be generalized to a one-to-many relationship.
*   **Replication:** A one-to-five relationship can be represented by five separate one-to-one relationships.
