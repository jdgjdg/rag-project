### Motivations for Transactions
There are two main motivations for transactions: concurrency and recovery from failures. In the event of a failure, the database must maintain both atomicity and durability, which are two of the four ACID properties.

### Types of Failures
Failures can be categorized in several ways. One type of failure involves receiving bad data. However, our focus here is primarily on transaction-related failures, such as transactions being aborted. This occurs when a user decides to cancel a transaction, such as at an ATM, or when a condition arises that requires a transaction to be rolled back. 

Additionally, we must consider hardware and system failures. Examples include the power being cut to the entire system or mechanical disk drive failure due to excessive usage.

### Solutions for Failure Recovery
For failures involving bad data, we have already examined solutions using constraints, triggers, and other programming techniques. 

For transaction-related failures, our focus will be on the use of logs. Other strategies we have discussed previously include:
*   **Data Replication:** Arranging multiple disks in a RAID configuration, replicating storage offsite, or replicating the database locally or offsite.
*   **Snapshot Archiving:** This is essentially version control applied to databases, where all data is timestamped and tracked to record when changes occurred and what the previous versions were.

We will specifically focus on logs as a method to implement transaction aborts and rollbacks.
